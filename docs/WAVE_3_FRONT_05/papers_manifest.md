## 1. Ingested Literature Corpus

### 1. Ostrom, Walker, & Gardner (1992)
- **Title**: *Covenants With and Without a Sword: Self-Governance is Possible*
- **Authors**: Elinor Ostrom, James Walker, Roy Gardner
- **Journal/Venue**: *The American Political Science Review*, Vol. 86, No. 2 (June 1992), pp. 404–417
- **Key Concepts**: Common Pool Resource (CPR) game; symmetric Nash extraction equilibrium vs. group optimal extraction; communication without enforcement ("covenants without a sword") vs. sanctions without communication ("sword without covenants") vs. endogenous governance ("covenants with an internal sword").
- **Engine Relevance**: Foundational empirical proof for Ostrom's ADICO syntax and endogenous authority matrices. Demonstrates that endogenous governance with targeted peer sanctioning achieves ~93% net yield in CPR dilemmas.
- **File**: [`ostrom_walker_gardner_covenants_sword_1992.pdf`](file:///c:/Users/Leonardo/001/00__DEV/HYPOSTASES/docs/WAVE_3_FRONT_05/papers/ostrom_walker_gardner_covenants_sword_1992.pdf)

### 2. Fehr & Gächter (2002)
- **Title**: *Altruistic punishment in humans*
- **Authors**: Ernst Fehr, Simon Gächter
- **Journal/Venue**: *Nature*, Vol. 415, No. 6868 (10 January 2002), pp. 137–140
- **Key Concepts**: Altruistic peer punishment definition; 4-player Public Goods Game (PGG); 1:3 cost-to-impact ratio ($1$ MU spent reduces target's payoff by $3$ MUs); rapid contribution decay without punishment vs. high sustainable cooperation under punishment options.
- **Engine Relevance**: Direct empirical calibration for `PUNISH_RESERVE_COST` in the ADICO engine. Sanction enforcement depletes internal capacity $c$ at a defined ratio while preserving collective governance state $g$.
- **File**: [`fehr_gaechter_altruistic_punishment_2002.pdf`](file:///c:/Users/Leonardo/001/00__DEV/HYPOSTASES/docs/WAVE_3_FRONT_05/papers/fehr_gaechter_altruistic_punishment_2002.pdf)

### 3. Bowles & Gintis (2002)
- **Title**: *Social Capital and Community Governance*
- **Authors**: Samuel Bowles, Herbert Gintis
- **Journal/Venue**: *The Economic Journal*, Vol. 112, No. 483 (November 2002), pp. F419–F436
- **Key Concepts**: Community governance as a solution to market and state failures under incomplete contracts; strong reciprocity; peer monitoring and informal sanctioning; parochialism and group homogeneity tipping.
- **Engine Relevance**: Provides theoretical grounding for informal Norms vs. formal Rules, and peer-to-peer authority matrices where monitoring and enforcement rights are distributed across members.
- **File**: [`bowles_gintis_social_capital_community_governance_2002.pdf`](file:///c:/Users/Leonardo/001/00__DEV/HYPOSTASES/docs/WAVE_3_FRONT_05/papers/bowles_gintis_social_capital_community_governance_2002.pdf)

### 4. Hurwicz (1973)
- **Title**: *The Design of Mechanisms for Resource Allocation* (Richard T. Ely Lecture)
- **Authors**: Leonid Hurwicz
- **Journal/Venue**: *The American Economic Review*, Vol. 63, No. 2 (May 1973), pp. 1–30
- **Key Concepts**: Formal mechanism design framework $(M, f, h)$; message space $M$; response function $f_i$; outcome function $h$; informational decentralization and privacy; Pareto-satisfactoriness; Hurwicz Impossibility Theorem.
- **Engine Relevance**: Architectural blueprint for how the `InstitutionAgent` handles message spaces, response functions, state transitions ($\sigma \to \sigma'$), and incentive compatibility.
- **File**: [`hurwicz_mechanisms_for_resource_allocation_1973.pdf`](file:///c:/Users/Leonardo/001/00__DEV/HYPOSTASES/docs/WAVE_3_FRONT_05/papers/hurwicz_mechanisms_for_resource_allocation_1973.pdf)

### 5. Nisan & Ronen (2001)
- **Title**: *Algorithmic Mechanism Design*
- **Authors**: Noam Nisan, Amir Ronen
- **Journal/Venue**: *Games and Economic Behavior*, 35(1-2), 2001, pp. 166–196
- **Key Concepts**: Algorithmic mechanism design; Vickrey-Clarke-Groves (VCG) truthful payment mechanisms; task scheduling makespan optimization; mechanisms with verification (post-execution checking enabling bonus/penalty functions).
- **Engine Relevance**: Core theoretical model for algorithmic task allocation, property rights, order matching in market institutions, and verification loops during tick execution.
